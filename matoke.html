Matoke

A staple with a soft, generous heart.

Matoke refers to East African highland cooking bananas and the dishes made from them. In Uganda, they are often steamed in banana leaves and gently mashed. This home-kitchen version pairs them with a simple groundnut sauce.

Good to know

Plant-based and gluten-free as written. Contains peanuts. Use firm, green East African cooking bananas if available; sweet dessert bananas are not a substitute.

Let’s cook.

Take your time, taste as you go. Tap each step to open or close it.

  1. Lightly oil your hands or wear food-safe gloves to avoid the sticky sap. Trim the banana ends, slit the skins lengthwise, and peel away with a small knife. Put the peeled bananas in a bowl of water while you finish the rest.

  2. Line a steamer basket with the banana leaf, if using, leaving room for steam to circulate. Add the drained bananas and sprinkle with 1 teaspoon salt. Set over simmering water, cover, and steam for 35–40 minutes, until a knife slides through easily. Check the water level regularly.

  3. Meanwhile, heat the oil in a saucepan. Soften the onion for 5 minutes, then add tomatoes and cook for 5 more. Whisk the peanut butter with 400 ml water, pour into the pan, and add ½ teaspoon salt. Simmer gently for 15–20 minutes, stirring often, until smooth and slightly thickened.

  4. Transfer the cooked bananas to a warm bowl and mash with a wooden spoon until soft but still textured. Taste the sauce and adjust the salt. Spoon the matoke onto plates and add the warm groundnut sauce and steamed greens.

The right banana matters

Matoke are starchy cooking bananas, not ripe eating bananas. If you cannot find them, ask an African or East African grocer for a suitable cooking variety. A banana leaf adds aroma, but a covered steamer works without one.

Bring it to the table

Serve matoke warm, with groundnut sauce alongside or spooned over. It can also accompany beans, meat stews, or vegetables. This is one pairing among many in Uganda’s varied food traditions.

Make a little extra

Refrigerate the matoke and sauce separately within 2 hours. Use within 3 days. Steam the matoke to reheat; warm the sauce in a saucepan, adding water as needed, until both are steaming hot throughout.
