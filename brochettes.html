Rwanda · East Africa

Brochettes

A little smoke, a little heat, a lot to share.

Brochettes are a familiar sight at Rwandan grills: skewered meat, often goat, cooked over charcoal. Onion, a modest marinade, and pili pili on the side make this a relaxed invitation to gather around the fire.

Good to know

Dairy-free. Use a gluten-free stock or water if needed. The pili pili accompaniment may be very hot; serve separately. Metal skewers work best over charcoal.

Let’s cook.

Take your time, taste as you go. Tap each step to open or close it.

  1. Combine the oil, tomato purée, garlic, lemon juice, water, salt, and black pepper in a bowl. Add the goat cubes and coat well. Cover and refrigerate for 20 minutes while you prepare the grill.

  2. Thread the goat onto 8 metal skewers, alternating with onion segments. Leave a little space between pieces so the heat can reach all sides. Discard any marinade that has touched raw meat.

  3. Heat a charcoal grill to medium-high with a cooler area to one side. Grill the skewers for 15–20 minutes, turning frequently. Move to cooler heat if they char too quickly. Check the thickest cubes reach at least 63°C, then rest for at least 3 minutes.

  4. Rest the brochettes on a warm clean platter. Serve with the separately grilled cooking bananas and pili pili sauce on the side. Slide the cooked meat off the hot skewers carefully before eating.

A Kigali-style gathering

Goat brochettes and grilled bananas are a beloved pairing in Rwanda, but marinades and accompaniments vary from grill to grill. Choose tender leg meat and cut evenly for the most reliable result.

Bring it to the table

Set out the skewers family-style with grilled bananas and a crunchy tomato salad. Pili pili is the fiery accent, not a requirement. A little sauce goes a long way.

Make a little extra

Remove the cooked meat from the skewers, cool promptly, and refrigerate within 2 hours. Keep up to 3 days. Reheat thoroughly in a covered pan with a small splash of water to keep it moist.
